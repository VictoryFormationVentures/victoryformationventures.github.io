What can be built · quick rules

  • HOME: SF-1, SF-2 and SF-3 lots allow up to 3 units; lots can be split to about 1,800 sf per unit (Phase 2).
  • Impervious cover 45%, building cover 40%, FAR 0.4 (up to 0.65 with 3 units and preservation bonus).
  • Heritage trees (24 in. and up) need a permit to remove; protected trees start at 19 in.
  • Fully Developed Floodplain is stricter than FEMA; check the Property Profile map, not just the FIRM.
  • STRs: Type 2 (non-owner-occupied) licenses are capped; assume no STR income in underwriting.
  • -NP, -CO and -NCCD suffixes change the numbers above; read the plan or ordinance.

Zoning in Austin · what the codes mean

How to read an Austin zone. A base district plus suffixes, e.g. SF-3-NP. The base sets use and lot rules; suffixes modify it: -NP Neighborhood Plan (plan-specific tweaks, check the plan), -CO Conditional Overlay (site-specific restrictions from the rezoning ordinance), -MU Mixed Use allowed, -V Vertical Mixed Use, -H Historic, -NCCD Neighborhood Conservation, -DB density bonus.

Residential bases. LA Lake Austin (1 acre min, steep-slope rules) · RR Rural Residence (1 acre) · SF-1 Large Lot (10,000 sf) · SF-2 Standard Lot (5,750 sf) · SF-3 Family Residence (5,750 sf; the most common infill zone, historically allowed duplexes) · SF-4A Small Lot (3,600 sf) · SF-5 Urban Family · SF-6 Townhouse & Condo · MF-1 through MF-6 multifamily by rising density · MH Mobile Home.

HOME amendments (2023–24) matter for infill. SF-1, SF-2 and SF-3 lots now allow up to 3 units per lot, and lots can be split down to roughly 1,800 sf per unit (HOME Phase 2), subject to 45% impervious cover, 40% building cover, and a 0.4 FAR (more with preservation or sustainability bonuses). Max height is 35 ft. This is why small-lot SF-3 in central Austin trades on a per-unit basis.

Commercial and other. LO Limited Office · GO General Office · LR Neighborhood Commercial · GR Community Commercial · CS General Commercial Services (the workhorse commercial zone; CS-MU-V allows residential over retail) · CBD and DMU downtown · LI Limited Industrial · P Public · PUD Planned Unit Development (its own rulebook, read the ordinance).

Watch for. Drinking Water Protection Zone and Barton Springs Zone (much lower impervious cover, critical environmental feature buffers), Hill Country Roadway and Lake Austin overlays, the Fully Developed Floodplain (stricter than FEMA), heritage tree rules (24"+ trunks), and compatibility standards next to single-family.

Travis County context

Travis County itself has no zoning outside city limits: unincorporated land is governed only by county subdivision, floodplain, and on-site sewage rules, plus TCEQ Edwards Aquifer rules on the west side. Inside Austin's ETJ the city's subdivision and water-quality rules apply but not its zoning. Most of the county's incorporated cities (West Lake Hills, Rollingwood, Lakeway, Bee Cave, Sunset Valley, Manor, Pflugerville) run their own zoning codes.

What can be built · quick rules

  • Impervious cover is capped by slope class and is usually the binding constraint, not setbacks.
  • Tree removal permits for most trees over 8 in.; heritage trees are effectively protected.
  • Setbacks are also measured from drainage ways and steep-slope areas.
  • No multifamily; duplexes not permitted in R-1.
  • Variances go to the Zoning and Planning Commission, so budget 60 to 90 days if the design pushes a limit.

Zoning in West Lake Hills · what the codes mean

The lay of the land. A wealthy, hilly, almost entirely single-family city west of MoPac inside the Eanes ISD. Lots are large, tree-covered and steep, and the city is protective: no multifamily to speak of, strict tree and slope rules, and a Zoning & Planning Commission that reviews most variances in person.

Districts. R-1 Single-Family (the bulk of the city, roughly 1-acre minimum lots), R-2 smaller-lot residential in older sections, plus limited C commercial along Bee Cave Road (FM 2244) and GUI government/utility. Most redevelopment is teardown-and-rebuild on existing lots rather than subdivision.

Watch for. Impervious cover caps tied to slope, setbacks measured from steep-slope and drainage areas, tree removal permits, and a lengthy variance calendar. Budget time for the Zoning & Planning Commission if the design pushes any limit.

Zoning in Lakeway · what the codes mean

The lay of the land. Lake Travis resort town turned suburb along RR 620, mostly built out, with infill lots, lake-view teardowns, and a few remaining PUD tracts. The GIS layer here is unusually good: it returns minimum lot area, width, setbacks and height for the district, which the lookup shows above.

Districts. R-1 Single-Family (the standard lot district), R-1A larger-lot single-family, R-2 duplex/two-family, R-3 and R-4 multifamily, R-5 zero-lot-line/patio homes, R-6 cluster housing, C-1 Commercial, O Office, GUI Government/Utility/Institutional, and many PUDs, each with its own ordinance.

Watch for. Lakeway sits in the Lake Travis watershed with LCRA and TCEQ rules on top of city code, so drainage and impervious cover reviews are real. Short-term rental rules are active politics here. Lake access lots may involve LCRA shoreline permits.

Zoning in Bee Cave · what the codes mean

The lay of the land. Retail hub at Hwy 71 and RR 620 (Hill Country Galleria) with expensive hillside residential around it. A very large share of the city is zoned PDD (Planned Development District), so the ordinance for the specific PDD governs, not the base district table.

Districts. SF-E Estate, SF-1/SF-2 single-family by lot size, MF multifamily, C-1/C-2 commercial, MU mixed-use, GUI, and PDD. The UDC (Section 3.2) is the reference.

Watch for. Hill Country Roadway-style scenic corridor standards on Hwy 71 and RR 620, Barton Springs/Edwards contributing zone water-quality rules, and steep-slope limits. Bee Cave's ETJ overlaps with Lakeway and Austin claims, so confirm jurisdiction first.

Zoning in Manor · what the codes mean

The lay of the land. Small east-side city on US 290 that has become one of the cheapest places to build new single-family in the metro, with large production-builder subdivisions, Tesla and Samsung commute traffic, and a big ETJ.

Zoning in Leander · what the codes mean

The lay of the land. One of the fastest-growing cities in the country, at the end of the CapMetro Red Line and 183A, with huge master-planned communities (Travisso, Bryson, Crystal Falls) and a big ETJ toward Lake Travis.

Composite zoning. Leander does not use simple districts. Each parcel's zoning is a combination of three components: a Use component (e.g. SFR rural, SFS suburban, SFU urban, SFC compact, SFL limited single-family; TF two-family; MF; LO/GC commercial; LI/HI industrial), a Site development component (lot size, setbacks, coverage), and an Architectural component. The lookup shows all three. Around the transit station the TOD SmartCode transects (T3 through T6) apply instead.

Watch for. PUDs override the composite rules; check the PUD name shown. Water is the constraint (Leander buys from BCRUA); confirm capacity early on larger tracts.
